# Jingeehas reverse-item audit V2

> **DESIGN PHASE ONLY**
> **NOT YET PSYCHOMETRICALLY VALIDATED**
> **NOT A CLINICAL OR PSYCHOLOGICAL DIAGNOSIS**
> **NOT READY FOR PRODUCTION PSYCHOMETRIC CLAIMS**

## Principle

Reverse scoring and reverse wording are different. A positively worded item can be reverse-scored for a barrier profile, but it does not necessarily measure the semantic opposite of the barrier. Mixed keying may reduce simple acquiescence while introducing comprehension burden, local dependence, and a wording-method factor. Reverse items should survive only if they add construct coverage and behave coherently in Mongolian cognitive interviews and factor analysis.

## Items not marked reverse-coded but directionally transformed

SE01–SE06 are naturally keyed confidence items. The scoring specification transforms the subscale for a higher-barrier profile, but individual items should **not** be called reverse-worded. Retain and display the natural confidence score as a strength; derive a barrier-direction view only through explicit scale-level metadata.

## Recommended design rule

- Avoid grammatical negatives and double negatives.
- Prefer concrete favorable behavior over “I did not…” wording.
- Do not force exactly one reverse item per construct.
- Include a wording-method factor or correlated uniqueness sensitivity model during validation.
- Compare models with and without positive items; do not delete only to raise alpha.
- If a favorable item measures a neighboring strength, either model that facet explicitly or remove it from the barrier subscale.
- Never reverse-code from text inspection; use immutable item-version metadata.

## Decision

No reverse-coded item is validated. Seven are provisionally suitable for continued testing, while EE06, EC06, UC06, and IM03 need wording/construct review before the frozen pilot. RR06, HA06, BI06, and IM05 have especially important cross-loading risks despite clean surface wording.
