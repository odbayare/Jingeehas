## Scope and method

All 56 rows in `ITEM_SPECIFICATION_BANK_V2.csv` were reviewed exactly once. This is a desk review, not expert content validation, cognitive interviewing, or empirical item analysis. The source CSV was not edited.

Risk codes: **L** low, **M** moderate, **H** high, **NA** not applicable. “Fit” is the preliminary content fit to the intended construct. “Scale” evaluates the specified response scale. Copyright review is a similarity-risk screen only, not rights clearance.

## Cross-cutting findings

- Social-desirability risk is highest for control, restraint, self-efficacy, and body-image content; nonjudgmental instructions and privacy assurances are necessary but will not eliminate bias.
- Positive/reverse-keyed items often measure capability, flexibility, or participation rather than a clean semantic opposite. They may create method factors.
- Several intended distinctions are scientifically plausible but vulnerable to cross-loading: EE–UC, EC–HA, UC–HS, RR–IM, RR–SE, BI–SE, and SE–IM.
- A 14-day period fits event-frequency items better than trait-like confidence/automaticity statements.
- Self-efficacy items appropriately use confidence, while EE02 is the clearest frequency-scale mismatch.
- No wording appears to copy a comparator item verbatim, but habit, self-efficacy, body-image, and food-preoccupation items require formal rights review against any comparator actually licensed for validation.
- Safety screening must remain separate. RR05 and intense loss-of-control/body-image language need expert distress review.
